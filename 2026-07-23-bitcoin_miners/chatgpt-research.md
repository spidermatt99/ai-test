# Bitcoin miners’ AI transitions

**Status: July 22, 2026. All financial figures are in U.S. dollars unless noted otherwise.**

“Pivot to AI” means different things across this group:

* **Data-center landlord/colocation:** the company supplies power, land, buildings and cooling to an AI tenant. Core Scientific, Applied Digital, TeraWulf, Cipher and CleanSpark fit mainly here.
* **GPU cloud operator:** the company owns GPUs and sells computing capacity. IREN, Bitdeer, WhiteFiber/Bit Digital and Hut 8’s Highrise business fit partly here.
* **Powered-land developer:** the company has energy and sites that might eventually support AI, but may not yet have a binding tenant. MARA is currently closest to this stage.

I judge success primarily by **capacity that is already live and billing, recurring AI/HPC revenue, signed creditworthy customers, committed financing and delivery progress**. I place less weight on multibillion-dollar “contract values,” because those generally represent ten to twenty years of future payments and may include tenant reimbursements, milestones, options or termination provisions. Also, “IT megawatts” are not directly comparable with gross utility megawatts.

## Overall position

| Company             | Current transition stage                                   | Assessment                                                    |
|---------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Core Scientific** | Operating AI infrastructure at scale                       | Strongest current revenue-mix conversion                      |
| **Applied Digital** | Operating AI infrastructure at scale                       | Among the strongest execution records                         |
| **TeraWulf**        | Meaningful operations plus major construction              | Strong, with substantial delivery still ahead                 |
| **IREN**            | Operating GPU cloud and scaling extremely rapidly          | Exceptional contracting; execution-intensive                  |
| **Bitdeer**         | Smaller but genuinely operating AI cloud                   | Credible, although mining remains much larger                 |
| **Riot**            | First major customer live; early recurring rent            | Promising but not yet a full business pivot                   |
| **Bit Digital**     | Operating through WhiteFiber subsidiary                    | Real transition, but parent structure is complex              |
| **Hut 8**           | Small live GPU operation plus enormous contracted pipeline | Strong commercial validation; most capacity not yet delivered |
| **Cipher Digital**  | Several financed leases under construction                 | Strong contracting; operating proof still pending             |
| **CleanSpark**      | First large lease signed; pre-revenue                      | Commercially validated but very early                         |
| **MARA**            | Acquisitions, partnerships and powered-land development    | Mostly strategic optionality so far                           |
| **Canaan**          | No material current AI-data-center pivot                   | Still fundamentally a mining-equipment and mining company     |

## 1. Hut 8 — HUT

### Bitcoin-mining history

Hut 8 Mining was established in 2017 and became one of the earliest institutionally scaled, publicly traded North American Bitcoin miners. In November 2023, it completed its all-stock merger with US Bitcoin Corp, adding a substantial U.S. operating and development platform. In 2025, Hut 8 created American Bitcoin as a dedicated mining business, and from April 2025 much of Hut 8’s self-mining activity was conducted through that platform. This effectively allowed the Hut 8 parent to emphasize energy infrastructure, data centers and managed compute rather than remaining only a Bitcoin miner. ([Hut 8][1])

### AI pivot and current stage

Hut 8’s first clearly operating AI product was a roughly **1,000-GPU NVIDIA H100 cluster**, placed into service in September 2024. It was supported by a five-year customer arrangement combining fixed payments with a revenue-sharing component. That operation provides real AI-compute experience, but it is modest compared with the parent’s newer data-center ambitions. ([Hut 8][2])

The much larger strategy is to become a developer and landlord for hyperscale AI campuses. By July 20, 2026, Hut 8 reported **949 MW of contracted critical-IT AI capacity**, representing approximately **1,330 MW of utility capacity**:

* River Bend: approximately 245 MW of IT capacity.
* First Beacon development: approximately 352 MW.
* Second Beacon development: approximately 352 MW.

The company reported approximately **$26.6 billion of base-term contract value** and more than **$1.75 billion of average annual net operating income** associated with the contracted portfolio. River Bend has a Google-backed lease structure, while Hut 8 has also closed or arranged billions of dollars of project financing for River Bend and Beacon. ([Hut 8][3])

### How successful has it been?

**Commercially, very successful; operationally, still early.** Hut 8 has secured tenants, credit support and construction financing at a scale that few former miners have matched. However, most of the 949 MW is still a construction and delivery story. Its demonstrated live AI operation remains the comparatively small H100 cluster.

The important distinction is that Hut 8 is becoming an **AI-infrastructure landlord and developer**, not principally a large proprietary GPU-cloud operator. Success now depends on completing campuses on time and on budget and converting contracted capacity into stable rent.

## 2. TeraWulf — WULF

### Bitcoin-mining history

TeraWulf began industrial-scale mining at its Lake Mariner campus in New York in March 2022 and started operations at the nuclear-powered Nautilus Cryptomine in Pennsylvania in early 2023. Its original investment case emphasized low-cost, domestically generated and relatively low-carbon electricity. ([TeraWulf Inc.][4])

A significant turning point came in 2024, when TeraWulf sold its 25% interest in Nautilus for approximately $92 million. The company directed part of the proceeds toward its first dedicated AI/HPC building at Lake Mariner, signaling that scarce capital would increasingly go toward data-center infrastructure rather than additional mining machines. ([TeraWulf Inc.][5])

### AI pivot and current stage

By the first quarter of 2026, TeraWulf had **60 MW of HPC capacity operating for Core42** and reported approximately **$21 million of quarterly HPC lease revenue**. This is important because it is recurring operating evidence rather than simply a future lease announcement. ([TeraWulf Inc.][6])

Its next phase is considerably larger:

* Approximately **360 MW** contracted to Fluidstack at Lake Mariner.
* An initial Fluidstack commitment of more than 200 MW carrying approximately **$3.7 billion of ten-year contract value**, with Google providing credit support.
* A July 2026 announcement for a **401-MW critical-IT campus for Anthropic** at the Justified Data project. TeraWulf reported approximately **$19 billion of twenty-year contract value**, with initial delivery targeted for the second half of 2027 and full capacity targeted for early 2028.
* TeraWulf is also monetizing part of the project through a Fluidstack-led joint-venture structure, reducing how much capital it must supply itself. ([TeraWulf Inc.][7])

### How successful has it been?

**TeraWulf is one of the strongest transitions in the group.** It already has meaningful HPC capacity generating lease revenue, while also securing very large counterparties and long-duration commitments.

The principal caveat is that the largest projects have not yet been delivered. The company must execute several hundred megawatts of construction, and its future economics will depend on financing costs, construction timing and the precise economics retained after joint-venture arrangements.

## 3. Riot Platforms — RIOT

### Bitcoin-mining history

Riot became one of the largest U.S. Bitcoin miners through the 2021 acquisition of Whinstone and the expansion of its Rockdale, Texas campus to approximately 700 MW. Mining was still overwhelmingly the core business in 2025: Riot produced 5,686 Bitcoin and reported approximately $576 million of Bitcoin-mining revenue for the year. ([Riot Platforms][8])

### AI pivot and current stage

Riot’s first significant AI customer is **AMD**. The initial agreement covered approximately 25 MW at Rockdale, and AMD subsequently exercised an option that brought contracted capacity to approximately **50 MW**, with the broader arrangement offering possible expansion to 200 MW. ([Riot Platforms][9])

Riot reported **$33.2 million of data-center revenue** in the first quarter of 2026. That headline needs qualification:

* Approximately **$32.2 million** was associated with tenant fit-out work.
* Only approximately **$0.9 million** was operating-lease revenue.
* Bitcoin-mining revenue was still approximately **$111.9 million**, with 1,473 Bitcoin produced during the quarter. ([Riot Platforms][10])

Tenant-improvement revenue helps fund and validate construction, but it is not equivalent to recurring rent. Therefore, the AMD agreement has not yet materially changed Riot’s recurring revenue mix.

### How successful has it been?

**The pivot is credible but early.** AMD is a high-quality anchor customer, and Riot has considerable Texas power and land that could support later phases. Nevertheless, the first-quarter numbers show that recurring AI lease revenue was still tiny compared with both mining revenue and fit-out reimbursements.

Riot has passed the “mere feasibility study” stage, but it has not yet reached the operating scale of Core Scientific, Applied Digital or TeraWulf.

## 4. Core Scientific — CORZ

### Bitcoin-mining history

Core Scientific’s founders began mining in 2012, and the company subsequently assembled a major U.S. mining and hosting footprint. It also has unusually deep historical experience with GPU infrastructure: it hosted CoreWeave GPU deployments between 2019 and 2022, well before the current generative-AI capacity boom. ([Core Scientific, Inc.][11])

After the 2022 crypto downturn and a Chapter 11 restructuring, Core Scientific emerged from bankruptcy in January 2024 and relisted its shares. At emergence, it controlled approximately 724 MW of active power capacity, giving it a large installed base that could be repurposed or expanded for HPC. ([Core Scientific, Inc.][12])

### AI pivot and current stage

In June 2024, Core Scientific signed a twelve-year agreement to supply approximately 200 MW to CoreWeave, building upon an earlier 16-MW deployment. Subsequent expansions brought the contracted CoreWeave portfolio to approximately **590 MW across six sites**, with the company citing approximately **$10.2 billion of projected revenue over twelve years**. ([Core Scientific, Inc.][13])

By the first quarter of 2026:

* Approximately **243 MW** was already billing.
* Colocation revenue was **$77.5 million**.
* Self-mining revenue was **$30.1 million**.
* The operating portfolio represented roughly **$350 million of annualized GAAP colocation revenue** at the quarter’s run rate.
* Capital expenditure was substantial—approximately $389 million during the quarter—but approximately $130 million was funded by CoreWeave. ([Core Scientific, Inc.][14])

This is the clearest example in the group of AI revenue already exceeding legacy mining revenue.

### How successful has it been?

**Core Scientific currently has the strongest demonstrated revenue transition.** It has moved beyond announcements and construction into hundreds of megawatts of billing capacity, and AI colocation is already the larger operating business.

Its biggest risk is **customer concentration**: nearly all of the major HPC portfolio is tied to CoreWeave. It is also a capital-intensive buildout. Nevertheless, based on live capacity and reported revenue rather than future contract value, Core Scientific is arguably the most advanced former miner in the transition.

## 5. Cipher Digital — CIFR

*Formerly Cipher Mining; ticker unchanged*

### Bitcoin-mining history

Cipher grew as an industrial Bitcoin miner, completing the Odessa facility and operating across four mining sites by 2023. At that point, it reported approximately 7.2 EH/s of mining capacity and was accumulating additional powered land, including the Black Pearl site. ([Cipher Digital Inc.][15])

In February 2026, the company changed its name to **Cipher Digital**, reflecting its intended shift toward large-scale data-center development. It also monetized portions of several mining ventures to help redirect capital toward AI facilities. ([Cipher Digital Inc.][16])

### AI pivot and current stage

Cipher’s most clearly disclosed project is Barber Lake:

* **168 MW of critical IT capacity**, or approximately 244 MW on a gross-power basis.
* A ten-year Fluidstack lease carrying approximately **$3 billion of reported contract value**.
* Google credit support of approximately **$1.4 billion**.
* Targeted delivery in September 2026. ([Cipher Digital Inc.][17])

The company has also arranged approximately **$2 billion of secured notes for Black Pearl**, signed another long-term hyperscaler campus lease and issued approximately **$810 million of notes for the third project, known as Stingray**. Not every tenant and capacity figure has been publicly disclosed. ([Cipher Digital Inc.][18])

Despite these achievements, first-quarter 2026 revenue was approximately $35 million and adjusted EBITDA was negative $48 million; the new AI campuses were not yet producing meaningful HPC lease revenue. ([Cipher Digital Inc.][19])

### How successful has it been?

**Cipher has been highly successful at signing leases and securing project financing, but it remains largely pre-revenue in AI.** Barber Lake’s targeted September 2026 delivery is an important near-term test.

Until the facilities are accepted by tenants and begin producing rent, Cipher should be viewed as a well-financed development story rather than a proven AI-data-center operator.

## 6. CleanSpark — CLSK

### Bitcoin-mining history

CleanSpark built a strategy around owning and operating its mining infrastructure rather than relying heavily on third-party hosting. It reached approximately **50 EH/s in June 2025**, making it one of the largest publicly traded Bitcoin miners, and emphasized that the fleet was fully self-operated. ([CleanSpark][20])

As recently as June 2026, mining remained the operating engine: CleanSpark produced 614 Bitcoin, had approximately 50 EH/s deployed and controlled a large contracted-power portfolio. ([CleanSpark][21])

### AI pivot and current stage

CleanSpark began publicly formalizing its AI-data-center strategy in late 2025, appointing dedicated leadership and evaluating sites, cooling technology and development partners. ([CleanSpark][22])

On July 14, 2026, it announced its first major AI lease:

* **175 MW of critical IT capacity**.
* A twenty-year triple-net lease.
* Approximately **$6.6 billion of base-term contract value**, rising to a company-reported $11.6 billion if extensions are exercised.
* Initial delivery expected in the fourth quarter of 2027.
* A separate exclusivity arrangement that could cover as much as 885 MW of additional Texas capacity. ([CleanSpark][23])

This is major commercial validation, but the company has acknowledged that development will require substantial additional capital and that construction delays could cause rent abatements or termination rights. ([CleanSpark][23])

### How successful has it been?

**CleanSpark has successfully moved from an AI concept to a binding large-scale lease, but it has not yet become an AI operator.** There is currently no meaningful AI revenue, and first delivery is not expected until late 2027.

The transition is therefore approximately where Cipher was before its projects approached completion: a strong tenant commitment, valuable power assets and considerable construction and financing risk.

## 7. MARA Holdings — MARA

### Bitcoin-mining history

MARA originated outside crypto as Marathon Patent Group and formally changed its name to Marathon Digital Holdings in March 2021 as Bitcoin mining became its primary business. It subsequently developed into one of the largest public miners and Bitcoin holders. ([MARA][24])

Mining still overwhelmingly dominates reported operations. In the first quarter of 2026, approximately **$172.2 million of MARA’s $174.6 million of total revenue** came from Bitcoin mining, with 2,247 Bitcoin produced. ([MARA][25])

### AI pivot and current stage

MARA has pursued several routes into AI and HPC:

1. **Exaion acquisition.** In February 2026, MARA acquired a controlling interest in Exaion, a provider of secure cloud and HPC services, for approximately $174.5 million. Exaion gives MARA a genuine operating foothold, although its contribution was not yet material to MARA’s consolidated results. ([MARA][26])
2. **Starwood partnership.** MARA partnered with Starwood to develop data-center sites, with Starwood expected to assist with development, construction, leasing and operations. MARA may contribute sites and retain ownership interests rather than funding every project alone. ([MARA][26])
3. **Power and land acquisitions.** MARA has pursued the Long Ridge power plant and a large HIF-related land portfolio. The HIF land could support up to approximately 1 GW initially and potentially 2 GW later, while Long Ridge may support several hundred megawatts. However, these announcements have referred to tenant interest rather than a completed hyperscale AI lease. ([MARA][27])

### How successful has it been?

**MARA has established strategic AI optionality but has not yet demonstrated a major operating pivot.** Exaion is real but small relative to MARA. Its larger assets are currently best viewed as powered-land and energy-development opportunities awaiting tenants, construction and financing.

Relative to Hut 8, TeraWulf or Cipher, MARA has not yet disclosed a comparably large binding AI lease. Bitcoin mining remains unmistakably the principal business.

## 8. Applied Digital — APLD

### Bitcoin-mining history

Applied Digital was built around large-scale data-center infrastructure and initially concentrated on hosting third-party cryptocurrency miners. Its legacy portfolio includes approximately **286 MW of fully contracted crypto-mining hosting capacity** across Jamestown and Ellendale. In its fiscal third quarter of 2026, that segment generated approximately **$37.5 million of revenue** and $13.9 million of segment operating profit. ([Applied Digital Corporation][28])

Unlike companies that operated large proprietary mining fleets, Applied Digital’s experience was primarily as an infrastructure owner and host. That made its move into AI colocation relatively natural.

### AI pivot and current stage

Applied Digital signed CoreWeave as the anchor tenant at its Polaris Forge 1 campus. The initial approximately 250-MW lease carried a company-reported value of roughly $7 billion over fifteen years; an additional 150 MW brought the campus to approximately **400 MW contracted**. ([Applied Digital Corporation][29])

By July 1, 2026, Applied Digital had delivered an additional 75-MW phase, bringing **live AI capacity at Polaris Forge 1 to 175 MW**. ([Applied Digital Corporation][28])

In the fiscal third quarter:

* HPC hosting revenue was approximately **$71 million**.
* Of that, approximately **$44.1 million was base rent**.
* Approximately $18.9 million was tenant-fit-out revenue.
* Approximately $8.1 million was pass-through revenue.
* Total company revenue was $126.6 million.
* The company still reported a net loss of approximately $100.9 million, illustrating the financing and depreciation burden of the buildout. ([Applied Digital Corporation][30])

Applied Digital has subsequently signed additional capacity, including a roughly 210-MW Delta Forge 2 commitment, and reports tens of billions of dollars of aggregate long-term contract value across its campus portfolio. ([Applied Digital Corporation][31])

### How successful has it been?

**Applied Digital is one of the two clearest operating successes, alongside Core Scientific.** It has delivered material capacity, reports substantial base rent and has shown that it can commission large AI buildings rather than merely announce them.

The main issues are heavy capital requirements, substantial debt and customer/project concentration. Nevertheless, the operating evidence is strong: AI/HPC revenue already exceeds its legacy crypto-hosting revenue.

## 9. Bitdeer Technologies — BTDR

### Bitcoin-mining history

Bitdeer developed as a vertically integrated crypto-infrastructure company, combining self-mining, hosting, data-center ownership and, increasingly, proprietary mining-chip development. As of June 2026, it reported:

* Approximately **3 GW of aggregate energy capacity**.
* Roughly **73 EH/s of self-mining capacity**.
* Another 15.9 EH/s associated with co-mining.
* Approximately 243,000 deployed mining machines.
* Production of 990 Bitcoin during June. ([Bitdeer Technologies Group][32])

Thus, unlike Core Scientific or Applied Digital, Bitdeer has not substantially exited or subordinated Bitcoin mining. Mining is still an enormous part of the business.

### AI pivot and current stage

Bitdeer began offering AI cloud services in 2023. By July 2026, it reported approximately **$76 million of annualized AI-cloud revenue at about 95% utilization**, with deployment of newer NVIDIA GB300 systems underway. ([Bitdeer Technologies Group][33])

It is also moving from GPU cloud into longer-term colocation:

* A ten-year lease for approximately **21.7 MW of IT capacity in Malaysia**, with handover targeted for the first quarter of 2027.
* A planned colocation arrangement at Tydal in Norway, subject to development and contractual conditions.
* Evaluation or conversion of selected former mining sites for AI workloads. ([Bitdeer Technologies Group][32])

The AI operation is real and utilized, but it remains small next to Bitdeer’s mining fleet. The parent also reported a significant net loss in the first quarter of 2026, although accounting and development expenses complicate direct interpretation. ([Bitdeer Technologies Group][34])

### How successful has it been?

**Bitdeer has a credible operating AI-cloud business, but not yet a company-transforming one.** Its utilization and ARR demonstrate actual customer demand rather than speculative powered land.

The next test is whether the Malaysia and Norway projects scale the AI business enough to become material relative to its 73-EH/s mining platform.

## 10. IREN — IREN

### Bitcoin-mining history

IREN, formerly known as Iris Energy, built large renewable-powered Bitcoin mining campuses, particularly in British Columbia and Texas. More recently, it has deliberately slowed or decommissioned portions of its ASIC-mining expansion so that power, buildings and capital can be directed toward AI cloud and data-center capacity. ([IREN][35])

Mining remained material in fiscal 2026, but the company has made the clearest strategic commitment in this group to prioritizing AI-cloud growth over continued maximum Bitcoin-hash-rate expansion.

### AI pivot and current stage

IREN’s initial AI cloud was only approximately **3 MW**, using company-owned GPUs. Its subsequent expansion is extraordinarily large:

* A five-year Microsoft agreement reported at approximately **$9.7 billion**, involving 200 MW of critical IT capacity at Childress.
* A five-year NVIDIA arrangement reported at approximately **$3.4 billion** for another 60 MW.
* Additional agreements announced in July 2026 with a reported value of approximately **$2.8 billion**.
* Customers reported by the company include Microsoft, NVIDIA, Perplexity, Figure AI, Together AI, Fluidstack, Fireworks AI, Fal and Hume. ([IREN][36])

IREN says it is moving from roughly 3 MW to approximately **480 MW of AI-cloud capacity being delivered during 2026**. It is targeting more than **$4 billion of annualized run-rate revenue by year-end**, with approximately 85% of that target already contracted. The company reported approximately $7.6 billion of cash at June 30, and customer prepayments covering roughly 45% of the associated GPU capital expenditure. ([markets.businessinsider.com][37])

Those numbers require an important qualification: the $4 billion is a **forward annualized run-rate target based on planned GPU commissioning, testing and customer acceptance**, not current GAAP revenue. Fiscal third-quarter 2026 total company revenue was $144.8 million, far below the prospective annual run rate, and the company reported a $247.8 million net loss. ([IREN][38])

### How successful has it been?

**IREN has arguably the strongest forward customer demand and funding story, but not yet the strongest delivered-revenue record.** Its contracts, customer prepayments and cash position substantially reduce financing uncertainty.

The execution challenge is unusually large: it must install and commission a vast quantity of high-end GPUs, satisfy multiple customers and manage rapid hardware depreciation. If the 2026 delivery plan is achieved, IREN could become the largest AI-cloud business in this peer group. Until then, Core Scientific and Applied Digital have more demonstrated operating revenue.

## 11. Bit Digital — BTBT

### Bitcoin-mining history

Bit Digital historically operated as a Bitcoin miner, using a relatively asset-light mix of hosted mining machines. Over time, it reduced its reliance on Bitcoin mining, built an HPC business and rebranded that operation as **WhiteFiber**, incorporating both GPU cloud and the Enovum data-center platform. ([Bit Digital][39])

The parent company’s current strategy is no longer a simple Bitcoin-to-AI pivot. It combines:

* An Ethereum treasury and staking strategy.
* A controlling interest in WhiteFiber.
* A much smaller residual digital-mining operation. ([Bit Digital][40])

### AI pivot and current stage

In the first quarter of 2026, Bit Digital consolidated WhiteFiber and reported:

* Total consolidated revenue of approximately **$27.9 million**.
* Cloud-service revenue of approximately **$16.8 million**.
* Colocation revenue of approximately **$4.8 million**.
* Digital-mining revenue of only approximately **$3.7 million**. ([Bit Digital][41])

At the WhiteFiber subsidiary itself, quarterly revenue was approximately **$21.9 million**, gross margin was 60.2%, adjusted EBITDA was approximately $3 million and net loss was approximately $12 million. Its MTL-3 facility was operating for Cerebras, while the initial 40-MW IT phase of its North Carolina project was progressing under an Nscale agreement. ([PR Newswire][42])

### How successful has it been?

**Bit Digital has genuinely moved away from Bitcoin mining, and its AI business is already operating.** On a revenue basis, cloud and colocation are now much more important than Bitcoin mining.

However, investors in BTBT own the parent, not a pure-play WhiteFiber security. The parent’s Ethereum strategy and the partially separate subsidiary structure make the exposure less direct than owning Core Scientific, Applied Digital or TeraWulf. WhiteFiber is promising, but still relatively small and not yet consistently profitable.

## 12. Canaan — CAN

### Bitcoin-mining history

Canaan is different from most of the list because it began primarily as a **Bitcoin-mining equipment manufacturer**, not as a large data-center landlord. Founded in 2013, it pioneered Avalon-branded ASIC mining machines and later developed a substantial self-mining operation. ([Canaan Inc.][43])

In the first quarter of 2026, Canaan reported:

* Approximately **$42.9 million of mining-equipment product revenue**.
* Approximately **$19.1 million of self-mining revenue**.
* Production of 257 Bitcoin.
* Roughly 11 EH/s of installed mining capacity.

It has also invested in additional mining projects, including acquiring interests in facilities previously associated with Cipher. ([Canaan Inc.][43])

### AI pivot and current stage

Canaan did introduce the **Kendryte K210 edge-AI chip in 2018**, an early RISC-V-based processor designed for local machine-learning inference. But this historical semiconductor product should not be confused with the current miner-to-AI-data-center strategy followed by Core Scientific, IREN or Applied Digital. ([Canaan Inc.][44])

Current company reporting continues to emphasize Avalon ASIC sales, mining services and self-mining. It does not disclose a material GPU-cloud, AI-colocation or hyperscale data-center business. ([Canaan Inc.][43])

### How successful has it been?

**Canaan has not made a meaningful contemporary pivot to AI infrastructure.** Its old edge-AI chip initiative is technologically interesting, but there is no evidence that it has become a material business or that Canaan is executing the same AI-data-center transition as the other companies.

For purposes of an investment thesis based on miners converting power into AI revenue, Canaan currently belongs outside the main peer group.

# Bottom line

### Best demonstrated transitions today

**Core Scientific and Applied Digital** have the strongest evidence of success because their AI facilities are live, billing at meaningful scale and already producing revenue comparable to or greater than their legacy crypto operations.

### Strongest combination of live operations and future growth

**TeraWulf** already has material HPC rent and an exceptionally large contracted pipeline. It has more current operating proof than most of the companies with multibillion-dollar announcements.

### Most ambitious GPU-cloud expansion

**IREN** has extraordinary customer and funding commitments. Its prospective scale may ultimately exceed the rest of the group, but much of the headline ARR depends on GPUs and data centers that are still being delivered and accepted.

### Real but smaller operating transitions

**Bitdeer, Riot and Bit Digital/WhiteFiber** have genuine customers and operating AI activity. Bitdeer remains mining-heavy; Riot’s recurring lease revenue is still small; and BTBT’s AI exposure comes through a majority-controlled subsidiary alongside an Ethereum strategy.

### Large contracted pipelines that still require delivery

**Hut 8, Cipher Digital and CleanSpark** have secured serious commercial commitments. Hut 8 has a small live GPU operation, while Cipher and CleanSpark are principally construction-stage stories. Their multibillion-dollar contract values should not be treated as revenue already earned.

### Mostly strategic optionality

**MARA** owns or is pursuing attractive energy and land assets and has acquired Exaion, but it has not yet disclosed a major binding hyperscale lease comparable with those of TeraWulf, Hut 8 or Cipher.

### Not currently an AI-transition company

**Canaan** remains a Bitcoin-mining-machine manufacturer and self-miner. Its historical AI chip does not amount to a current AI-infrastructure pivot.
