# Bitcoin miner Iren’s pivot to AI data centres is helping it attract more retail investment than Microsoft and Alphabet

> ## Excerpt
> The Australian data centre operator and bitcoin miner has secured AI search engine Perplexity as a customer, helping it lift revenue forecasts.

---
Iren’s pivot from bitcoin mining to artificial intelligence services provider is gaining rapid momentum, with the group securing $4 billion in new contracts and drawing retail investors to its shares at a faster rate than tech giants Microsoft and Amazon.

The Nasdaq-listed company founded by Australian brothers Daniel and Will Roberts intends to dedicate its existing and future data centres in North America, Australia and Europe solely to cloud services that power AI.

Daniel Roberts, co-founder and co-CEO of Iren, has secured several new customers for its AI services, including Perplexity. Oscar Colman

Several United States-based companies have signed up as new customers, including AI search engine Perplexity, robotics company Figure AI, and image generator Fal AI, adding up to $US2.8 billion ($4 billion) worth of new deals.

Iren has also ramped up its revenue projections and now expects to make $US4 billion from selling AI cloud services in 2027, up from the $US3.7 billion forecast in May.

The new target assumes Iren will have 480 megawatts of capacity by the end of calendar 2026, up from just 3MW a year ago, said Daniel Roberts. The target doesn’t include other revenue that Iren expects to make from an additional 730MW of capacity planned to be deployed by the end of 2027.

The company, whose biggest customer is Microsoft, is targeting 1.2 gigawatts of capacity by 2027 as it chases new contracts with hyperscalers, AI developers and other organisations.

“Our vertically integrated AI cloud platform is scaling at pace,” Roberts said, after the company acknowledged demand was exceeding its available capacity.

Iren’s shares soared almost 20 per cent in New York on Monday, closing at $US40.20, more than double its share price 12 months ago.

Individual shareholders have pumped more than $US50 million into the company in the first half of July, exceeding the value of shares purchased in Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Tesla, according to data from London-headquartered analytics firm Vanda Research, cited by _The Wall Street Journal._

The four big technology companies are part of the so-called [“magnificent seven” group of American stocks,](https://www.afr.com/wealth/investing/magnificent-seven-s-stock-market-dominance-shows-signs-of-cracking-20260113-p5nts7) which also include Nvidia, Meta and Apple, and have previously led the US sharemarket.

But 2026 is on track to be the worst year for the “mag seven” since 2022, as investors worry over whether their massive investments in AI will generate sufficient returns, according to investment research group Morningstar.

Other companies founded by Australians that provide computer equipment to process AI services are also snapping up new contracts.

Sharon AI, which is also listed on the Nasdaq and expects to float on the Australian Securities Exchange later this year, said on Friday that it would provide computing services from New Zealand for the first time after signing a new $US1.32 billion five-year cloud computing agreement with a global AI lab.

The new contract reflects a shift towards dedicated AI infrastructure across the Asia-Pacific region, said Sharon AI chief executive and co-founder James Manning.

“As AI moves from pilots to production, organisations are securing long-term access to sovereign, high-performance compute as a strategic capability. ”

Iren has been mining bitcoin – using computers to verify digital tokens – since 2019 and listed on the Nasdaq in November 2021. But to capitalise on the boom in AI, it has diversified into building data centres dedicated to processing information used in AI services.

It has three data centre sites in Texas, and three in British Columbia, Canada. Additional sites are being developed [near the regional town of Bundey in South Australia](https://www.afr.com/companies/financial-services/iren-plans-10-billion-south-australian-ai-data-centre-20260603-p603o9), as well as in the US state of Oklahoma.

Iren’s acquisition of Spanish data centre developer Ingenostrum in May added sites in Spain that are expected to start operating from 2028, giving the company a foothold in Europe for future expansion.

Bitcoin mining still accounted for most of Iren’s $US501 million in annual revenue for the year to June 30, 2025, with just $US16.4 million coming from AI services.

On Monday, Iren said it had $US7.6 billion in cash and cash equivalents as at June 30, 2026. It hasn’t yet announced a date for reporting its 2025-26 annual results.
