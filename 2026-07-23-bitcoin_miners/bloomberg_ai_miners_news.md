# Crypto Miners' $10 Billion AI Pivot Is Yielding Wall Street's Best Returns

**By Antigravity Tech & Markets**
**July 23, 2026**

For anyone who was able to see which way the wind was blowing on the day ChatGPT was launched on November 30, 2022, and bought into VanEck's SMH semiconductor exchange-traded fund, they would be sitting on phenomenal gains of over 428%. 

But if they had instead taken that money and invested it in a basket of the top 10 Bitcoin miners, they would have made an astonishing 1,282%.

The biggest names in mining the original cryptocurrency have become Wall Street darlings this year, with gains outstripping even Silicon Valley tech giants. The only catch is that they no longer mine Bitcoin.

Like so many firms this year, these companies are cashing in on the 2026 artificial intelligence craze. Crypto miners were well positioned for this with their high-performance computing (HPC) and GPU clouds, which are now being repurposed to serve AI clients.

IREN is the latest big winner in this cohort. It saw share gains of 20% in a single day last week after revealing a fresh slate of AI-hosting contracts worth $2.8 billion.



The great Bitcoin mining capitulation is minting a new class of Wall Street darlings, provided they no longer actually mine Bitcoin. 

Shares of Iris Energy Ltd., now rebranded as IREN, surged as much as 20% in a single day this week after revealing a fresh $2.8 billion slate of AI-hosting contracts, the latest crescendo in a spectacular multi-year pivot that has transformed former crypto operators into the backbone of the artificial intelligence boom. 

IREN’s stock has now skyrocketed more than 3,300% from its 2023 cyclical lows, a stunning reversal of fortune for a sector that was on life support during the last crypto winter. And it’s hardly alone. A cohort of digital asset miners that repurposed their power-hungry infrastructure for high-performance computing (HPC) and GPU clouds are completely decoupling from the very cryptocurrency they were built to harvest.

The divergence this year is staggering. While Bitcoin has slumped roughly 24% year-to-date in 2026, struggling to regain its footing, the miners who leased their gigawatts to tech giants are posting triple-digit gains. Hut 8 Corp. has surged 139% since January, Riot Platforms is up 85%, and TeraWulf Inc. and Cipher Digital have both climbed over 65%. 

“It’s the most lucrative real estate arbitrage of the decade,” said one data center infrastructure analyst. “These companies secured cheap, massive power contracts in Texas, New York, and British Columbia to run ASICs. Now, hyperscalers like Microsoft, Google, and Nvidia need that exact infrastructure desperately. The miners are simply ripping out the crypto rigs and plugging in H100s.”

### The Pivot Premium

The financial engineering behind the pivot is producing astronomical returns when measured from the depths of the 2023 crypto crash. 

TeraWulf, which traded for around 50 cents a share in early 2023, is up more than 3,500% after shedding its legacy crypto baggage and converting its zero-carbon Lake Mariner power infrastructure for AI colocation leases. Cipher Digital, which recently secured Google credit support for a massive new development, has posted a mind-bending 3,700% return from its 2023 trough. 

IREN itself reached a peak gain of over 6,300% earlier this year following monumental multibillion-dollar agreements with Microsoft and Nvidia, before settling into its current, still-stratospheric valuation.

By contrast, companies that stubbornly clung to their core crypto-mining identity have been left behind. Canaan Inc., which continues to focus on Bitcoin mining and equipment manufacturing, has plummeted nearly 88% over the same period, illustrating the harsh penalty markets are applying to pure-play crypto operators.

### From Hashes to Hyperscalers

The transformation hasn't been cheap. Retrofitting warehouses designed for crude Bitcoin ASICs into Tier-3 data centers capable of cooling tightly packed Nvidia server racks requires billions in capital expenditure. But Wall Street has been more than willing to foot the bill, treating these firms less like volatile crypto proxies and more like digital infrastructure utilities with decades of recurring revenue. 

Core Scientific, which famously went bankrupt in 2022, emerged and immediately inked a 12-year, $10 billion contract with CoreWeave. Its stock is up over 390% since its AI transition began in earnest, with AI colocation revenue now actively dwarfing its self-mining operations. 

For investors, the math has become remarkably simple. Bitcoin mining yields unpredictable rewards tied to a volatile asset and an ever-increasing global hash rate. AI hosting yields 20-year triple-net leases backed by the most creditworthy tech companies on Earth.

As IREN's latest 20% pop demonstrates, the market’s appetite for the latter shows no signs of cooling. In 2026, the best way to profit from the Bitcoin ecosystem is to stop mining it.
