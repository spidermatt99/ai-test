# Crypto Traders Mark Down Their SpaceX-Sized Bet on Anthropic

**An AI-stock rout and post-election worries have provided the backdrop for a selloff in bets on a company that has yet to begin public trading.**

*Financial-news draft, not a published Bloomberg News article. Prepared November 11, 2026; derivative prices are from the November 11, 04:42 UTC snapshot. Source links and editorial checks follow the story.*

Crypto traders were pricing Anthropic for a stock-market debut on the scale of SpaceX. They are now marking down that bet before the artificial-intelligence company has even started trading publicly.

Anthropic’s contract on TradeXYZ, a derivatives market built on the Hyperliquid blockchain, fell almost 20% over Nov. 5 and Nov. 6 as AI stocks slumped and investors assessed the fallout from the US midterm elections. On a fixed diluted-share basis, that knocked about $416 billion off its implied equity valuation in two days.

The contract has since recovered some of that decline. Its $104.25 mark price early Wednesday implied a valuation of about $1.88 trillion, compared with roughly $2.12 trillion at its highest daily closing price in late October. The rebound leaves the price about 11% below that peak, rather than signaling an uninterrupted retreat.

The calculations multiply TradeXYZ’s price by approximately 18.013 billion diluted shares derived from the capitalization table in Anthropic’s Nov. 10 amended prospectus. Applying that same count retrospectively isolates changes in the market’s price expectations; it is not a claim that traders knew the final capital structure when they placed their bets.

Even after that retreat, the contract implied a valuation roughly 34% above the top of Anthropic’s filed IPO range. The proposed $70-to-$78 share price corresponds to approximately $1.26 trillion to $1.41 trillion using the same diluted-share basis, with a midpoint of $1.33 trillion.

Bankers are now weighing an increase to $1.5 trillion or more, Bloomberg News reported, citing the Financial Times. At $1.88 trillion, the derivatives-implied valuation would still stand about 25% above the $1.5 trillion threshold. The report describes plans under consideration, not a formally revised offering range. The deal had orders covering all available shares when it launched, Bloomberg reported.

SpaceX sets a different benchmark for the size of the offering itself: its June IPO raised a record $75 billion. Anthropic’s announced sale of 825 million shares would total as much as $64.4 billion at the top of the filed range, before the underwriters’ option. Whether it can challenge the record depends on the final offer price and number of shares sold, not simply on a high valuation in derivatives markets.

What the contracts provide is something private-market investors rarely get: a continuously changing, publicly visible price for expectations that ordinarily emerge through funding rounds, secondary transactions and bankers’ conversations with prospective buyers. The revaluation is playing out on trading screens before Anthropic has an official stock price.

These are bets on Anthropic, not shares in it. Buyers receive neither an IPO allocation nor ownership rights. But with about $296 million in combined notional open interest across Hyperliquid’s three active Anthropic markets and roughly $253 million in reported trading volume over the preceding 24 hours, the market is large enough to offer more than an occasional private-market price indication. Entropy and TradeXYZ account for almost all that activity.

## AI’s Market Reckoning

The retreat has unfolded alongside a broader reassessment of the AI trade.

On Nov. 5, the Nasdaq Composite slumped 5.5% and the S&P 500 lost 4.4%. Nvidia fell 9.3% and Advanced Micro Devices dropped 17.3%, according to the Associated Press. The election added to existing concerns about the expense of financing the AI buildout: Democratic gains raised the prospect of greater resistance to data-center construction amid voter concerns about AI and electricity bills, the AP reported.

For Anthropic, that puts the proposed debut against a broader question about the industry's outlook. Investors must weigh surging demand for AI against the cost of supplying it and the possibility that political opposition could constrain the infrastructure on which further growth depends.

The almost 20% slide in TradeXYZ’s Anthropic contract over Nov. 5 and Nov. 6 coincided with that public-market shock. The timing does not establish that politics or the stock-market selloff caused the move, but it shows private-company speculation was not insulated from the wider retreat.

Nor does the decline amount to a simple bet that AI demand is fading. Anthropic’s amended IPO filing reported $33.2 billion in revenue for the first nine months of 2026, against $2.5 billion a year earlier. Its annualized revenue run rate reached about $81 billion at the end of October — a measure of current sales pace, not revenue already earned over a year.

The expense of pursuing that growth is equally striking. Compute and infrastructure expenses totaled $18.8 billion in the first nine months, and the company recorded a $1.26 billion operating loss. Its much larger $104 billion net loss included a $94.3 billion charge from changes in the fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and other financing instruments; it should not be mistaken for operating cash burn.

The product race is still accelerating. Anthropic introduced Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 on Nov. 9, saying they offered faster output at lower prices than their predecessors. For investors, that leaves a harder question than whether the technology is improving: how much of the economic benefit will accrue to model developers competing to make it cheaper?

## A Price, Not a Promise

The contracts offer a way to express that uncertainty around the clock. They also introduce their own complications.

TradeXYZ’s ANT represents the anticipated price of one Class A share, not a direct quote for the entire company. The $1.88 trillion figure is therefore a calculation from its market price and the prospectus, not a company valuation published by the trading venue.

The share-count calculation sums the adjusted Class A and Class N shares, options after the filing’s treasury-stock adjustment, and restricted stock units in the prospectus table. It includes the table’s convertible-note and public-benefit-related adjustments and excludes the two control shares carrying no economic rights. It retains the filing’s assumptions, including a $74 offering price and no exercise of the underwriters’ option, rather than recalculating the capital structure at each derivatives price.

That is a diluted, fixed-share-count comparison, not basic market capitalization or a final IPO valuation. Final offering terms could change the denominator. Other venues, including Entropy, use their own valuation units and provisional share assumptions, making a blended cross-exchange figure harder to interpret.

Leverage, funding payments, liquidity and operator-set pricing rules also affect the signal. Hundreds of millions of dollars in derivative exposure are not equivalent to investors committing that amount to buy IPO shares.

## The IPO Scorecard

Earlier offerings show both the promise and the limits of these markets.

Before AI chipmaker Cerebras Systems began Nasdaq trading on May 14, its Hyperliquid contract traded at a volume-weighted average of $354.54 during the final hour before the opening print, according to Talos research. The stock opened at $350: a miss of just 1.3%.

But the IPO itself had been priced at $185. The derivatives were much closer to the price available once public trading began than to the price paid by investors receiving an allocation from the underwriters.

SpaceX was less flattering. Talos’s November review put its pre-IPO perpetuals around $176. The company sold shares at $135 and opened at $150 on June 12, CNBC reported. The quoted derivatives level was therefore about 30% above the offer price and 17% above the first public trade.

Talos also found SpaceX perpetuals trading within 2% of the underlying stock during market hours a week after the debut. That demonstrates convergence once a public reference price exists, not that the pre-listing forecast was accurate.

The comparisons are not a standardized forecasting test: the Cerebras figure is a final-hour weighted average, while the SpaceX figure is an approximate pre-listing level. Two high-profile listings cannot establish how reliably the contracts will price the next one.

For Anthropic, the message is narrower than a definitive verdict on what the company is worth. Traders have marked down their expectations, then raised them again — all while its bankers are still gathering orders.

The IPO will supply an offer price and, after that, a public trading price. Crypto markets are making the argument over both visible before either exists.

### SpaceX comparison and midterm fact-check

- **Midterms:** Rechecked the [AP November 5 report](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/national-international/november-5-stock-market/4170982/). It explicitly links Democratic electoral gains with investor concerns about possible pauses in data-center construction, alongside existing financing-cost and valuation concerns. No trader interviews or order-flow attribution have been obtained.
- **Largest IPO:** [CNBC's June 12 SpaceX coverage](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/12/spacex-ipo-spcx-live-updates.html) reports **$75B raised**. Largest IPO normally refers to offering proceeds, not issuer equity valuation. Anthropic's **825M × $78 = $64.35B** base offering, or **948.75M × $78 = $74.0025B** with the entire announced underwriters' option. The concurrent $26B private placement is not part of the public offering and should not be added to claim an IPO proceeds record.
- At the announced 825M-share base size, an offer price above approximately **$90.91** would exceed $75B in gross offering proceeds. This is conditional arithmetic, not a prediction or reported target. Do not retroactively treat the November 10 offered-share count as a term known to October traders. A perp quote above this threshold also does not prove the offer will be priced there: perps may anticipate an aftermarket premium.
- **Valuation comparability:** SpaceX's reported **$1.77T** offer-price capitalization used basic Class A/B shares; this story's Anthropic **$1.88T** uses a diluted basis. They are not harmonized denominators. “SpaceX-sized” is a broad description of the scale of the debut, not an exact comparative valuation claim. The draft deliberately compares the public-offering proceeds separately.
- The latest XYZ snapshot has rebounded and remains above the filed IPO range. The evidence supports **paring back expectations**, not declaring that a record-sized offering is impossible or that the company has lost the same dollar amount from an actual public market capitalization.
