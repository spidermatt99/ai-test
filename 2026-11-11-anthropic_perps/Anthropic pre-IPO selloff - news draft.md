# Anthropic’s Pre-IPO Selloff Plays Out on Crypto Trading Screens

**Derivatives expose a falling valuation before Nasdaq trading begins, though a recent rebound shows how quickly the verdict can change.**

*Financial-news draft, not a published Bloomberg News article. Prepared November 11, 2026; derivative prices are from the November 11, 04:42 UTC snapshot. Source links and editorial checks follow the story.*

Anthropic hasn’t started trading on Nasdaq. In a corner of the crypto market, investors have already watched hundreds of billions of dollars disappear from its implied valuation — and some of it come back.

A perpetual-futures contract offered by Entropy on the Hyperliquid blockchain valued the maker of Claude at about $1.81 trillion early Wednesday, roughly 21% below its highest daily closing valuation of $2.28 trillion on Sept. 9. The difference: about $470 billion, before the artificial-intelligence company has sold its IPO shares.

A separate contract from TradeXYZ, which tracks an anticipated share price rather than total valuation, told a similar story. It fell almost 20% between the Nov. 4 and Nov. 6 daily closes as AI stocks tumbled, before recovering to a $104.25 mark price. That remained about 11% below its highest daily close in late October — but 34% above the top of Anthropic’s proposed $70-to-$78 offering range.

The recovery matters. Entropy’s mark was about 19% above its Friday close. And Bloomberg News reported, citing the Financial Times, that Anthropic’s bankers were considering lifting the offering’s valuation to $1.5 trillion or more after strong investor demand. The deal had orders covering all available shares when it launched, Bloomberg reported. The derivatives aren’t delivering a one-way verdict against the company.

They are providing something private-market investors rarely get: a continuously changing, publicly visible price for expectations that ordinarily emerge through funding rounds, secondary transactions and bankers’ conversations with prospective buyers.

These are bets on Anthropic, not shares in it. Buyers receive neither an IPO allocation nor ownership rights. But with about $296 million in combined notional open interest across Hyperliquid’s three active Anthropic markets and roughly $253 million in reported trading volume over the preceding 24 hours, the market is large enough to offer more than an occasional private-market price indication. Entropy and TradeXYZ account for almost all that activity.

## AI’s Market Reckoning

The retreat has unfolded alongside a broader reassessment of the AI trade.

On Nov. 5, the Nasdaq Composite slumped 5.5% and the S&P 500 lost 4.4%. Nvidia fell 9.3% and Advanced Micro Devices dropped 17.3%, according to the Associated Press. Investors were confronting both the cost of financing the data-center boom and the prospect of greater political resistance to its expansion after Democratic gains in the midterm elections.

The Anthropic contracts moved in the same direction. Entropy’s daily closing valuation fell from about $1.87 trillion on Nov. 4 to $1.53 trillion two days later, an 18% decline. That coincidence does not establish that politics or the stock-market selloff caused the move, but it shows private-company speculation was not insulated from the public-market shock.

Nor does the decline amount to a simple bet that AI demand is fading. Anthropic’s amended IPO filing reported $33.2 billion in revenue for the first nine months of 2026, against $2.5 billion a year earlier. Its annualized revenue run rate reached about $81 billion at the end of October — a measure of current sales pace, not revenue already earned over a year.

The expense of pursuing that growth is equally striking. Compute and infrastructure expenses totaled $18.8 billion in the first nine months, and the company recorded a $1.26 billion operating loss. Its much larger $104 billion net loss included a $94.3 billion charge from changes in the fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and other financing instruments; it should not be mistaken for operating cash burn.

The product race is still accelerating. Anthropic introduced Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 on Nov. 9, saying they offered faster output at lower prices than their predecessors. For investors, that leaves a harder question than whether the technology is improving: how much of the economic benefit will accrue to model developers competing to make it cheaper?

## A Price, Not a Promise

The contracts offer a way to express that uncertainty around the clock. They also introduce their own complications.

TradeXYZ’s ANT represents the anticipated price of one Class A share. Entropy’s ANTH is quoted in billions of dollars of implied fully diluted valuation. Comparing the two requires a share-count assumption — and changing that assumption can change a headline valuation without any movement in the quoted share price.

At $104.25, TradeXYZ implies about $1.36 trillion using the filing’s basic post-offering Class A and Class N economic-share count. Applying Entropy’s published provisional diluted estimate instead produces roughly $1.82 trillion. That second number is a normalization for comparison, not an independently verified valuation published by TradeXYZ. Entropy’s estimated count remains provisional and has not been reconciled here with the filing’s capitalization bridge.

Those differences make changes within the same contract easier to interpret than a blended valuation across exchanges. Even then, leverage, funding payments, liquidity and operator-set pricing rules can affect the signal. Hundreds of millions of dollars in derivative exposure are not equivalent to investors committing that amount to buy IPO shares.

## The IPO Scorecard

Earlier offerings show both the promise and the limits of these markets.

Before AI chipmaker Cerebras Systems began Nasdaq trading on May 14, its Hyperliquid contract traded at a volume-weighted average of $354.54 during the final hour before the opening print, according to Talos research. The stock opened at $350: a miss of just 1.3%.

But the IPO itself had been priced at $185. The derivatives were much closer to the price available once public trading began than to the price paid by investors receiving an allocation from the underwriters.

SpaceX was less flattering. Talos’s November review put its pre-IPO perpetuals around $176. The company sold shares at $135 and opened at $150 on June 12, CNBC reported. The quoted derivatives level was therefore about 30% above the offer price and 17% above the first public trade.

Talos also found SpaceX perpetuals trading within 2% of the underlying stock during market hours a week after the debut. That demonstrates convergence once a public reference price exists, not that the pre-listing forecast was accurate.

The comparisons are not a standardized forecasting test: the Cerebras figure is a final-hour weighted average, while the SpaceX figure is an approximate pre-listing level. Two high-profile listings cannot establish how reliably the contracts will price the next one.

For Anthropic, the message is narrower than a definitive verdict on what the company is worth. Traders have marked down their expectations, then raised them again — all while its bankers are still gathering orders.

The IPO will supply an offer price and, after that, a public trading price. Crypto markets are making the argument over both visible before either exists.
