The Monetary Authority of Singapore wants local crypto investors to know that not even the best-known companies in the city-state are safe bets.

On June 26, the central bank added decentralized derivatives exchange Hyperliquid to its investor alert list, marking the third addition to the list from the crypto industry this year. Along with Bybit, also added in June, and KuCoin, added in February, the trio are among the best-known industry players to attract the wary gaze of the city's largest financial regulator.

Hyperliquid has recently become a hot topic in crypto circles. Its blockchain is home to perpetual futures contracts that track everything from commodities such as oil and gold to stocks like SpaceX, which launched on the platform before the company had the largest-ever initial public offering. Traders have turned to the 24/7 exchange to trade outside traditional market hours and gain exposure to products they otherwise wouldn't have access to.

Besides being the biggest names to land on the alert list since Binance in 2021, Hyperliquid, Bybit and KuCoin all have something else in common: a notable Singpaore presence. Hyperliquid set up operations in Singapore in 2024. KuCoin uprooted to the city following a crypto crackdown in China in 2018, while Bybit was founded by Singaporean Ben Zhou that same year. 

The MAS does not offer specific reasons for why any specific company gets put on the list, which serves as a warning to investors but is not accompanied by any regulatory action. As it said in the [case of Bybit](bbg://news/stories/TGTQJIKIJHBE), the MAS said entities on the list "may have been wrongly perceived as being licensed or authorised" to operate in the city. Hyperliquid was listed following an assessment of "documentary evidence," the regulator said.

The investor alert list has been increasingly populated with crypto-related firms, but many of them are flagged for being scams or appearing to impersonate other legitimate businesses. For the past two years, roughly a quarter of companies added to the list have marketed crypto-related businesses. Yet even among the larger of these companies, few were attracting the kind of business as the three listed this year.

MEXC, which was listed in March 2025, is perhaps the only one operating at the scale of Bybit, with each having 24-hour trading volume above $1.5 billion, according to CoinGecko data. All cryptocurrency exchanges pale in comparison to Binance, which sees more than $7.5 billion in 24-hour trading volume.

Also listed last year was Kyber Network, a decentralized protocol and exchange operator. Kyber and Hyperliquid are two of the only DeFi firms to land on the list. One of the implicit promises of decentralized technology — which runs without any central authority, unlike exchanges such as Binance — is that authorities cannot kill it. This idea is what attracted many to Bitcoin in its early days.

Bitcoin, though, is not a company. It has no base of operations, unlike Hyperliquid, which has an office registered in an industrial building five miles north of Singapore's central business district.
