# STATEMENT OF CLAIM

## A. OVERVIEW OF THE PLAINTIFFS' CLAIMS

1. Binance is the world's largest crypto exchange, with more than 310 million registered users globally. Binance's flagship crypto payment products include Binance Pay and Binance Card.
2. The Defendants, as RedotPay's substantial shareholders, orchestrated a scheme to divert Binance's users and transactions to RedotPay, and in turn injuring the Binance Group.
3. Pursuant to their fraudulent scheme, the Defendants induced the Plaintiffs to allow Binance Pay to be used within RedotPay, by falsely representing, and repeatedly assuring, that Binance Pay funds would not be used to top up the RedotPay Card, which competes with Binance Card.
4. Those representations and assurances were untrue and fraudulently made by the Defendants while the RedotPay business, with their endorsement, pursued a consistent and coordinated course of conduct to the contrary. This fraudulent scheme channelled Binance users and funds into RedotPay, generating substantial profits to the Defendants and increasing the valuation of RedotPay's planned IPO to approximately US$4 billion, while causing Binance loss of at least US$472,788,690.

## C. BACKGROUND

14. Binance is the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange handling over US$65 billion in average daily volume which accounts for approximately 40% of all trading on centralised cryptocurrency exchanges globally. As at the end of 2025, Binance had more than 310 million registered users globally.

15. As to the Binance Products:
    15.1. since at least 2021, Binance Pay and Binance Card have been used extensively in the course of trade internationally in relation to cryptocurrency exchange services, digital payment services, and cryptocurrency-linked card services.
    15.2. internationally, over 21 million merchants accept Binance Pay as a payment option on their websites or through the use of QR codes; and Binance Pay has processed transactions exceeding US$250 billion since its launch.
    15.3. both Binance Pay and Binance Card are advertised and promoted through the Binance website, Binance App, and other channels.

16. A main product of Redotpay is the RedotPay card ("RedotPay Card"), which is a payment card on either the Visa or Mastercard network.
    16.1. Like the Binance Card, the RedotPay Card offers a real-time stablecoin payment system, which instantly deducts stablecoins (such as USDT or USDC) from the user's wallet when shopping. Accordingly, the RedotPay Card is a competing product to the Binance Card.
    16.2. The RedotPay Card is available in both physical and virtual form, and allows users to make payments for retail purchases with merchants that accept Visa or Mastercard networks, using crypto/stablecoin held in users' RedotPay account.
    16.3. Users of the RedotPay platform manage their usage of the RedotPay Card through the RedotPay application ("RedotPay App"), which can be used to top up their RedotPay Card, as well as other ancillary functions such as transferring/receiving crypto and use of a mixed crypto/fiat multi-currency wallet.
    16.4. The RedotPay App was developed and is operated and/or owned by Red Dot Technology. Users' crypto that is deposited with RedotPay are held in custody by Red Dot Trust.

17. On 2 November 2023, DTL and Red Dot Technology entered into an agreement ("First Agreement") such that RedotPay was able to make Binance Pay available as a payment method to its users.

18. The integration with Binance Pay enabled deposits on RedotPay to take substantially less time to process, and rendered it as a real alternative to traditional crypto cards.

19. On 24 April 2024, DTL wrote to Red Dot Technology to terminate the First Agreement by 24 May 2024 after the Binance Group discovered that RedotPay had been allowing crypto assets originating from Binance Pay to be used not just to make deposits into user wallets, but also to top up the crypto prepaid RedotPay Cards. The Binance Group considered there was a risk that card-related volumes would be diverted from Binance Card to RedotPay, and resolved not to allow any merchants to top up prepaid and debit cards using crypto assets originating from Binance Pay.

## D. THE REPRESENTATIONS AND THE AGREEMENTS

20. In September 2024, Mr Chan approached the Binance Group with a view to re-establishing the commercial relationship and re-introducing Binance Pay into RedotPay.

21. In the commercial discussions which took place thereafter, the Binance Group (including Nest, DTL and Chaintecs) were represented by Mr Nick Fong (Operations Lead for Binance Pay) ("Mr Fong") and Mr Jonathan Lim (the Global Head of Binance Pay and Binance Card at the material time).

22. On 26 September 2024, Mr Gao (through the name "Michael G") and Mr Yao (through the name "Troy"), exchanged the following telegram messages with Mr Fong (through the name "Nic"). Mr Chan was also part of the telegram group where these messages were exchanged:
    > **Nic:** ... using Binance Pay to topup funds to your wallet is fine, but these funds cannot be used for the users' card consumption. meaning you need to segregate these funds for your other services (i see that you have send/receive/transfer)
    > **Troy:** Thanks for your explanation, I think we can build better user experience and services for users through BinancePay. We will not provide card recharge service.
    > **Nic:** thanks. how would the funds in your wallet operate? So you will segregate the funds within your wallet from binance pay for other services (and not card), am I right?
    > **Troy:** Yes, your understanding is correct
    > **Michael G:** We will top up the funds from Binance Pay to the user's wallet for,
    > 1) global fiat payout to users under the same name or third party merchants for trade settlement
    > 2) token inapp transfer
    > 3) marketplace for RedotPay branded goods (to be launched soon)
    > There will be no topup into the card

23. By reason of the aforesaid telegram messages, the Defendants made the following representations to Mr Fong (the "Telegram Representations"):
    23.1. funds received via Binance Pay would be used solely for payouts in traditional fiat currencies (such as the US dollar) globally, in-app token transfers, and the marketplace for RedotPay-branded goods (the "Three Permitted Uses");
    23.2. Binance Pay funds would not be used to top up any card product (the "Prohibited Use"); and
    23.3. Binance Pay funds would be strictly segregated from funds used in connection with RedotPay Card.

24. In the case of Mr Chan, he made the Telegram Representations impliedly by reason of the fact that he was part of the relevant telegram group but did not clarify or correct any of the Telegram Representations. Accordingly, he had endorsed the Telegram Representations by conduct.

25. Induced by and in reliance upon the Telegram Representations, DTL entered into a Binance Pay Direct Merchant Agreement with Red Dot Technology on 7 October 2024 (the "DMA") for the purposes of re-enabling Binance Pay to be included as a payment method for RedotPay's customers on RedotPay's stores, website and/or mobile app.

26. The Telegram Representations were incorporated into the terms of the DMA (and subsequently the Binance Pay Master Aggregator Agreement between DTL and Red Dot Trust dated 25 March 2025 (the "MAA", together with the DMA, the "Agreements")):
    26.1. the Three Permitted Uses of Binance Pay funds were enshrined as the definition of the Red Dot Technology's principal business in Schedule 1 to the DMA;
    26.2. Red Dot Technology agreed that it "shall... ensure that it will use the Binance Pay Services only in connection with its principal business as provided in Schedule 1" to the DMA;
    26.3. the prohibition against card top-up activities was made express in Clause 4.2(f)(v) of the DMA and Clause 3.3(n)(v) of the MAA. Each clause prohibited the use of Binance Pay in connection with "high risk industries (including, but not limited to, pre-paid, debit and credit cards top-ups)";
    26.4. both Red Dot Technology and Red Dot Trust agreed that "[i]n case [they] offer services, businesses or features that are prohibited by Binance (including, but not limited to, activities listed under 4.2(f) [of the DMA or 3.3(n) of the MAA]..." (i.e., "pre-paid, debit and credit cards top-ups", amongst other things), they "will ensure that it displays a notice to (via pop up notice etc.) the Shared Customers that Binance Pay is not available in connection with such services, businesses and features...".

27. On 4 November 2024, Mr Fong (through the username @nf889901) noted a spike in volume in RedotPay's account and exchanged the following telegram messages with Mr Chan (through the username @heresjc):
    > **@nf889901:** hi @heresjc - we saw the spike in vol for your account. just to check they are all related to remittance?
    > **@nf889901:** also wallet product?
    > **@heresjc:** Hi @nf889901, funds deposited via Binance Pay are solely for wallet top-ups.
    > **@heresjc:** Confirm there is no top up into the card, as this is a credit card issued under Money Lender licence in Hong Kong.

28. Mr Chan represented through the aforesaid telegram message that the Telegram Representations remained true after the execution of the DMA and would continue to remain true (the "Assurance Representations").

29. On 25 March 2025, induced by and in reliance upon the representations set out above (including the Telegram Representations and the Assurance Representations), DTL entered into the MAA with Red Dot Trust whereby, inter alia, DTL appointed Red Dot Trust to recruit merchants for Binance Pay services' acceptance and perform applicable Know-Your-Business due diligence on the merchants.

30. Like the DMA, the MAA incorporated into its terms the express representations made by RedotPay's founders described above, including:
    30.1. Red Dot Trust agreed in Clause 3.3(n)(v) of the MAA that it "shall ensure that none of the Merchants is engaged in any of the following businesses and/or activities...high risk industries (including, but not limited to, cryptocurrency exchanges, coin swap services, pre-paid, debit and credit cards top-ups) as determined by [DTL] in its sole discretion"; and
    30.2. Red Dot Trust agreed in Clause 3.3(l) of the MAA that "[i]n case the Merchant offers services, businesses or features that are prohibited by [DTL] (including, but not limited to, activities listed under [3.3(n) of the MAA]..." i.e., "pre-paid, debit and credit cards top-ups", amongst other things, [Red Dot Trust] "will ensure that the Merchant displays a notice (e.g., via a pop-up notice etc.) to the User that Binance Pay is not available in connection with such services, businesses and features...".

31. The Telegram Representations and Assurance Representations are collectively referred to as the "Representations".

32. In or around early 2026, the Binance Group implemented an internal reorganisation of its business units. As part of such reorganisation, on 19 January 2026, the Agreements were assigned from DTL to Nest.

33. As a result of the conclusion of the MAA and DMA:
    33.1. RedotPay was able to market the integration as a flagship product feature and materially increased the volume of cryptocurrency flowing through its platforms.
    33.2. RedotPay featured Binance prominently as a "Partner" on its public-facing website to date.
    33.3. more than US$247.7 million in user funds flowed from Binance Pay through RedotPay as master aggregator under the MAA, and a further US$56.7 million flowed from Binance Pay directly to RedotPay under the DMA. RedotPay's userbase doubled from 3 million to 6 million in the course of 2025 alone, with nearly half a million unique users joining RedotPay from Binance over the term of the Agreements.
    33.4. the RedotPay Group completed successive fundraising rounds, reportedly raising US$40 million in its Series A funding round in March 2025, and a further US$107 million in its Series B funding round in November 2025.
    33.5. the RedotPay Group attracted institutional investors including Accel, BCAP, Coinbase Ventures, Galaxy, Goodwater, Hongshan, Lightspeed, Pantera, and Vertex. The RedotPay Group is now preparing for an initial public offering at a valuation of approximately US$4 billion.

## E. FRAUDULENT MISREPRESENTATION

34. Since March 2026, the Binance Group has discovered that RedotPay Group had been allowing and encouraging Binance Pay funds to be used, without segregation, for the Prohibited Use within RedotPay, including card top-ups for RedotPay Card.
    
    **PARTICULARS**
    (1) RedotPay had implemented functionality on the RedotPay platform by which Binance Pay funds could be and was used, without segregation, for the Prohibited Use including allowing users to top up, load, recharge, or otherwise "pre-pay" the RedotPay Cards.
    (2) In particular, after selecting the deposit function in RedotPay, users of RedotPay had an option to choose Binance Pay as an option to "recharge" or "top-up" a selected RedotPay Card, following which the "recharge" or "top-up" could be confirmed as a deposit from within the RedotPay App.
    (3) Users of RedotPay published reviews, tutorials and videos on various websites and YouTube channels demonstrating how to use Binance Pay to top up the RedotPay Card, and presented such functionality as an approved and integrated feature of the Binance and RedotPay platforms. The RedotPay Group must have had knowledge of the aforesaid publications but did not take any steps to clarify or correct the incorrect perception.
    (4) The Plaintiffs reserve their rights to supplement with further details pending discovery and interrogatories.

35. Both Red Dot Technology and Red Dot Trust were instrumental in the RedotPay Group's allowing use of Binance Pay funds for the Prohibited Use.
    35.1. Red Dot Technology was responsible for the development, operation, and ownership of the RedotPay App. Red Dot Technology implemented the technical functionality enabling Binance Pay funds to top up RedotPay Cards.
    35.2. Red Dot Trust, as custodian of users' crypto assets held by the RedotPay platform, received, held, and processed the crypto assets deposited via Binance Pay in connection with the prohibited card top-up transactions, thereby operationally facilitating each such transaction.

36. By reason of the matters pleaded in paragraph 34 above, the Representations were (at the time they were made) and are still false and untrue.
    
    **PARTICULARS OF FALSITY**
    36.1. The funds received via Binance Pay were not used solely for the Three Permitted Uses, but also the Prohibited Use.
    36.2. There was no segregation of Binance Pay funds from funds used in connection with RedotPay Card.
    36.3. Insofar as the Representations were statements of intention, the Defendants did not in fact have the intention stated at the time when the Representations were made by reason of the matters pleaded in paragraph 37 below.

37. The Representations were made by the Defendants fraudulently, in that they knew that the Representations were false, or did not have an honest belief that the Representations were true, and/or reckless in that they did not care whether the Representations were true or false:
    
    **PARTICULARS OF FRAUD**
    37.1. The permission of the use of Binance Pay funds for the Prohibited Use was deliberate and systematic.
    37.2. The Defendants knew or must have known (i) that the truth of the Representations was essential to the Binance Group's willingness to re-engage and were a central feature of the Agreements, and (ii) of the terms in the DMA and MAA which gave effect to the Representations as pleaded in paragraphs 26 and 30 above.
        a. DTL terminated the First Agreement with the RedotPay Group after it discovered that RedotPay had been allowing crypto assets originating from Binance Pay to be used to top up prepaid and debit cards (i.e. the Prohibited Use).
        b. The Representations were direct responses to specific questions from the Binance Group's personnel, in full knowledge that re-onboarding was contingent on the Binance Group's satisfaction that card top-up activity would not recur and upon the Binance Group's reliance upon the truthfulness of the Representations.
    37.3. Accordingly, the Defendants would have ensured that RedotPay comply with the Representations had they acted honestly.
    37.4. The RedotPay Group, including Red Dot Technology and Red Dot Trust, were at all material times under common ownership, management, and/or control, with the Defendants as its substantial ultimate owners. Further, the Defendants were also at all material times executives or directors of entities within the RedotPay Group and must have an intimate knowledge of the operations and affairs of RedotPay.
    37.5. By reason of the aforesaid, the permission of the use of Binance Pay funds for the Prohibited Use must have been approved by the Defendants, or implemented with their knowledge.
    37.6. The Defendants never indicated that RedotPay would have difficulty implementing a system in place to prevent the Prohibited Use or any such system was not implemented or effective, whether at the time the Representations were made, or at all.
    37.7. The Defendants as ultimate substantial beneficial shareholders of the RedotPay Group benefited significantly through RedotPay's engagement in the Prohibited Use, and had an incentive to knowingly and/or recklessly make false Representations to conclude the MAA and DMA. Paragraph 33 is repeated.

38. DTL relied on the Representations and were induced by them to enter into the Agreements. But for the Representations, DTL would not have entered into the Agreements.

39. In the premises set out above, the Defendants are liable for damages in the tort of deceit and/or for fraudulent misrepresentation.

## F. UNLAWFUL MEANS CONSPIRACY

40. Further or alternatively, the Plaintiffs claim against the Defendants for unlawful means conspiracy.

41. The Defendants acted in combination and in concert with each other with an intention to injure or cause financial loss to the Binance Group, including the Plaintiffs, by way of unlawful means, namely fraudulent misrepresentation as pleaded in Section E above.

42. The Defendants' agreement and/or combination was to induce DTL to enter into the Agreements, in order to divert Binance's users and their crypto and funds into RedotPay's competing ecosystem and the RedotPay Card, at the expense of the Binance Group.

43. In particular, Binance Pay's ability to allow payments to be made directly to merchants overlaps with the functionality of the RedotPay Cards. The same customer who chooses to use a RedotPay Card to make a purchase from a merchant will not, within that same transaction, consider using Binance Pay (or Binance Card) to make that payment. By presenting the RedotPay Card as supported by or integrated with Binance Pay, RedotPay diverted Binance users away from the Binance Products, including Binance Card, and into transacting with RedotPay's competing card products.

44. The agreement and/or combination is to be inferred from the following acts and matters:
    44.1. the Defendants' control and influence over Red Dot Technology and Red Dot Trust, as pleaded in paragraph 10 to 13 above;
    44.2. the overt acts accrued out by the Defendants, including the Representations which were made fraudulently, as well as their endorsement of the deliberate, consistent and coordinated acts carried out by Red Dot Technology and Red Dot Trust in respect of the use to which Binance Pay funds would be used (i.e. permitting the Prohibited Use in RedotPay), as pleaded in sections D and E above; and
    44.3. the benefits the Defendants, through the dividends and increase in value of their shares in Rabbit7 Holding, would all enjoy from the flow of funds and customers from Binance as well as the significant increase in valuation upon a successful initial public offering (as pleaded in paragraph 33.5 above).

45. Each of the Defendants had an intention to injure the Plaintiffs, to be inferred from (i) each of Mr Gao, Mr Chan and Mr Yao's unlawful and overt acts pleaded above and (ii) the significant harm that the Plaintiffs would suffer if funds and customers were directed to an unsanctioned and competing product line.

## G. LOSS AND DAMAGE

46. By reason of the matters pleaded in Sections E and F above, the Plaintiffs have suffered loss and damage to be assessed, including but not limited to:
    46.1. the lost profits from Binance users who were channelled to RedotPay;
    46.2. costs incurred or to be incurred by Binance Card to acquire new users to replace the users channelled to RedotPay;
    46.3. additional marketing and promotional costs to be incurred by Binance Card as a result of a more difficult and competitive market arising from the improved profile of the RedotPay Card after the conspiracy committed by the Defendants;
    46.4. costs incurred or to be incurred by Binance to inform or clarify to the market it did not and would not allow the use of Binance Pay funds to top up the RedotPay Cards; and
    46.5. the expenditure in the investigation of the unlawful acts committed by the Defendants.

47. Without prejudice to the generality of the aforesaid, by way of illustration, the Plaintiffs estimate that at least 471,845 users were channelled from Binance Card to RedotPay during the terms of the Agreements. On the assumption that the lifetime value of a customer is US$925 per user, and the customer acquisition cost is US$77 per user, the Plaintiffs have suffered loss in the sum of at least US$472,788,690 (471,845 x US$(77+925)). The Plaintiffs reserve the right to plead further particulars as to their loss and damage.
