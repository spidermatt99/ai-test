# Hong Kong Crypto Unicorn RedotPay Churns Executives in IPO Drive

RedotPay, a Hong Kong startup in the stablecoin payments space, is contending with executive turnover and sensitivities around its connections to mainland China as it seeks to raise up to $150 million just months after its last funding round.

The company is in talks with firms about the new funding, according to people familiar with the matter who asked not to be named discussing confidential information. It comes after RedotPay raised over $150 million across two rounds in September and December from backers including the venture arms of crypto giants [Coinbase Global Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/COIN:US) and [Circle Internet Group Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CRCL:US)

A spokesperson said RedotPay remains open to strategically valuable investors but there is no urgency for funds given strong cash flow.

The startup has attracted a billion-dollar valuation from venture backers as it seeks to emulate [Revolut](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1304654D:LN), the British fintech that has [aspirations to become](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-25/revolut-weighs-share-sale-later-this-year-on-pre-ipo-demand) a $100 billion company. RedotPay is targeting a valuation of more than $4 billion through a public listing in the US as soon as this year.

RedotPay’s hard-charging style has created a work culture that some employees decided was too much. Interviews with eleven people close to the firm, including investors and current and former staff, show at least five senior hires lasted less than 12 months — the role of compliance chief turned over twice — and employees were routinely asked to work until 10 p.m. for weeks at a time.

The company is currently pursuing its IPO plans without a chief financial officer. It is also grappling with a sensitive situation in China, which in February further [tightened](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-06/china-bars-unapproved-issuance-of-yuan-linked-stablecoins-abroad) longstanding curbs on digital assets.

Still, RedotPay continues to grow. Annualized total payment volume surpassed $10 billion in December, more than doubling year-on-year, while revenues doubled to $158 million, according to investor materials reviewed by Bloomberg. It has more than six million users in over 100 countries.

### Breaking Through

RedotPay has already won over a bevy of blue-chip backers including Lightspeed Venture Partners, the venture capital firm with $40 billion in assets.

“The rate of product development is really fast,” said Pachara Lawjindakul, the Lightspeed partner who led RedotPay’s Series A round. “That’s what it takes to break through the noise.”

The first version of RedotPay debuted in June 2023, with a virtual card launched the following month. The core product is an app-and-card-combo. Users hold their stablecoins in the app and can use the Visa-issued card to pay for a coffee, for example, or shop online. The app also offers yield on stablecoin holdings and remittance products.

Stablecoins are a kind of cryptocurrency engineered to maintain a price pegged to that of an established asset, most often the US dollar. Institutional interest in digital dollars surged last year after the US pushed through new rules governing their usage. The total market value of all stablecoins in circulation rose roughly 50% to about $310 billion in 2025, while transaction volumes soared.

RedotPay’s holding company is Rabbit7 Holding (BVI) Ltd., a British Virgin Islands entity so-named because its seven co-founders incorporated it in 2023, the Year of the Rabbit in the Chinese zodiac.

### ‘Just Like Rabbits’

They hoped to “execute and move fast, just like rabbits,” the RedotPay spokesperson said. “It’s one of the key elements of the company’s culture.”

One of the co-founders is Michael Gao, a former banker and fintech entrepreneur who is RedotPay’s chief executive officer. Gao often tells staff to look to emulate Revolut, which began as a simple travel card and rapidly expanded into products including stock trading, crypto, insurance and mortgages, one of the people said.

Another key co-founder, Dawei Yuan, was a former co-founder and operations director at crypto exchange Huobi, which was set up in Beijing in 2013 and is now known as HTX. Yuan is one of RedotPay’s biggest shareholders, according to people familiar with the matter.

Then there’s Troy Yao, another former Huobi veteran who today serves as RedotPay’s chief operating officer. Up until last year, Yao ran a team of software engineers in China — first in Beijing and later in Shenzhen.

That team was at times subjected to a work schedule that saw staff routinely finishing work at 10 p.m. for weeks at a time, according to people familiar with the matter and a screen-shot of internal exchanges reviewed by Bloomberg. Some of the people say it resembled the “996” system — prevalent among Chinese tech firms — under which staff are expected to work from 9 a.m. to 9 p.m. six days a week.

“At our annual all-staff meeting in January 2026, our CEO promoted work-life balance and discouraged overtime,” RedotPay’s spokesperson said, adding that its teams have been consolidated to improve collaboration and efficiency.

At least five staff hired in senior positions over the past year lasted less than 12 months at the company, the people familiar said. Two people in the compliance chief role left in the space of roughly a year, while the growth and product teams have also been impacted, they said.

“As we transition from an early-stage startup to a unicorn, we are evolving our organizational structure and talent pool to support our ongoing growth trajectory,” RedotPay’s spokesperson said.

The company’s finance division is overseen by a head of finance and corporate development lead. “We may appoint a CFO at a later stage as the need arises,” the spokesperson said.

### China Links

Many of RedotPay’s venture backers are American, including the likes of Goodwater Capital and Accel.

Gaorong Ventures, the Beijing-based tech investment firm, and Haun Ventures are also among RedotPay’s investors, the investor materials prepared in December show. But Gaorong’s participation wasn’t more widely disclosed due to sensitivities around Beijing’s position on crypto, a person familiar with the matter said. Haun wasn’t disclosed because its investment was small.

RedotPay’s spokesperson said they are unable to comment on these investments due to confidentiality considerations. Gaorong and Haun didn’t respond to requests for comment.

Beijing has for years maintained a sweeping ban on crypto usage. RedotPay doesn’t serve customers in mainland China, according to its [website](https://helpcenter.redotpay.com/en/articles/10339263-redotpay-unsupported-countries-regions "Website").

The Shenzhen team that Troy Yao oversaw was relocated to Hong Kong at some point in 2025 after some of the startup’s US investors expressed concerns over its location, people familiar said.

Today, RedotPay’s 250 employees are predominantly based in its Hong Kong office, with some compliance and customer support staff in Kuala Lumpur.

The company continues to grow rapidly, the investor materials show.

Growth has been driven mainly by “product innovations that solved the pain points of customers using stablecoins for daily transactions,” its spokesperson said.

A public listing “is one of our considerations now,” they added. “We are always evaluating capital market opportunities.”
