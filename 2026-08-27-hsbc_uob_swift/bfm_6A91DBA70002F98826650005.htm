Trump Crypto Ally David Bailey Rebuilds After 9T% Stock Collapse

A year ago, David Bailey appeared to have the Midas touch. The crypto entrepreneur who helped win Donald Trump over to Bitcoin had raised nearly $800 million for a public company built to stockpile the cryptocurrency, while his hedge fund had returned 640% backing similar bets.

Since then, shares of Bailey’s Nakamoto Inc. have collapsed about 9T% from their peak, part of a broader rout in digital-asset treasury companies that has wiped out the premium investors once paid to own Bitcoin through publicly traded vehicles. Bailey, who remains in touch with the White House, is now trying to prove there is a viable business underneath the collapse.

“It’s been a very rough year for us,” Bailey said in an interview at this year’s Bitcoin Asia conference. “We bought Bitcoin at the top, and then Bitcoin performed very poorly.”

The immediate problem is survival. Nakamoto now trades well below the value of its Bitcoin holdings, making it far harder to issue stock to buy more of the cryptocurrency. Bailey is instead turning to a more conventional source of firepower: businesses that generate cash.

Nakamoto is hunting for another acquisition that can improve earnings and help finance future Bitcoin purchases without repeatedly issuing shares or taking on more debt. It is also prioritizing repurchases of its own discounted stock over adding to its Bitcoin holdings.

“We’re looking at businesses that can help us improve our income and our cash flow at the company. We’re looking at businesses that have strategic value to Bitcoin as a whole.”

Nakamoto’s shares fell so far that the company carried out a 1-for-40 reverse split in May to regain compliance with Nasdaq’s minimum bid-price requirement. The collapse has also fueled anger among retail investors and Bitcoin devotees who bought into the company during the boom.

Bailey has kept buying shares himself and says the company is nowhere near finished. His political access has proved more durable: he said he remains in contact with senior White House officials, including about outreach to the Bitcoin community ahead of the midterms.
Bitcoin has recently climbed back toward $80,000 after falling below $60,000 earlier this year.

Whether that marks the end of the bear market is uncertain. For Bailey, the more pressing question is whether Nakamoto can survive long enough to benefit from a recovery — and whether investors will ever again pay a premium for a company built largely around owning more Bitcoin.
