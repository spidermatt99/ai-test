By now, the digital-asset Treasury bust is well known. The pressing question now is what the survivors are turning into.

The answer, increasingly, looks less like a Bitcoin treasury and more like a strange new breed of conglomerate: part holding company, part lender, part asset manager, part energy or mining play. After months of trading below the value of their crypto, some of the industry’s better-known names are trying to build actual earnings around balance sheets that were once sold largely on the promise of more Bitcoin.

Twenty One Capital is hunting for profitable operating companies. Sora Ventures is raising $1 billion to help its portfolio companies build businesses from Thailand to South Korea. Nakamoto is looking for acquisitions that can generate enough cash to fund future Bitcoin purchases without leaning so heavily on new shares or debt.

The first generation of DATs proved that public companies could buy Bitcoin. The second showed they could use buoyant equity markets to buy even more. Now comes the harder version: can they turn those balance sheets into businesses that produce cash when the premium is gone and retail investors beat a retreat.

Mitty Chang, chief growth officer at Sora Ventures, calls it “DAT version 3.0.”
