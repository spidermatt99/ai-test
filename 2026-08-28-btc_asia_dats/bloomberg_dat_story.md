# Crypto’s 'Treasury' Pioneers Are Flailing for a Second Act Even as Bitcoin Surges

**By Bloomberg News**
**August 28, 2026**

Bitcoin’s return to the $80,000 mark has ignited celebrations across the broader cryptocurrency market, marking the digital asset's best month in nearly two years. At Bitcoin Asia, the region's largest industry gathering, the energy is palpable. 

"The sentiment is positive—Bitcoin is no longer a fringe hobby interest," said Brandon Green, CEO of BTC Inc., noting that the week's rally has only reinforced the momentum coming into the conference. "We're seeing a clear shift from 'number go up' enthusiasm to a focus on infrastructure, corporate treasury strategies, and regulatory frameworks."

But for a specific cohort of companies that built their entire identity around hoarding the token, the mood is decidedly more desperate. 

Digital Asset Treasury companies—or "DATs"—are scrambling to redefine their business models. Initially hailed as innovators for aggressively issuing debt and equity to accumulate Bitcoin on their balance sheets, these companies are now facing a harsh reality: investors are no longer willing to pay a premium just to hold a proxy for the cryptocurrency. 

Despite the recent crypto rally, the DAT sector is still reeling from a massive wipeout. Over $80 billion has been erased from the market capitalizations of top Bitcoin treasury companies since July 2025. Many of these stocks continue to trade at steep discounts to their Net Asset Value (NAV), signaling a collapse of market faith in the so-called "DAT 1.0" model. 

The irony is that even the highest-profile attempts to replicate the Bitcoin-treasury model have shown how difficult the transition can be. Nakamoto, founded by David Bailey—a prominent figure in Donald Trump’s embrace of crypto who served as an adviser during his presidential campaign—sold about $20 million of Bitcoin in March to fund operations. The company unloaded 284 Bitcoin at an average price of about $70,000, well below its reported weighted-average acquisition price of $118,171.

That fire sale offers a striking counterpoint to the bullish case Bailey has publicly made. In February, amid a prolonged slide in prices, Bailey told Bloomberg he remained "eager to buy Bitcoin." 

Nakamoto’s capitulation underscores the central tension now facing the sector: believing Bitcoin will ultimately rise is not the same thing as having enough cash flow to survive until it does. 

### The Pivot to 'DAT 3.0'

"The market is telling us that there was a misallocation of capital," said Raphael Zagury, CEO of Twenty One Capital, which holds the world's second-largest Bitcoin corporate treasury. "Raising capital and leveraging the capital is okay, but it's a transaction. The way we're looking at this is much more longer-term."

Faced with a model that many analysts now view as doomed, the industry's survivors are franticly pivoting to what insiders call "DAT 3.0." The new strategy? Actually operating a business.

"We’re going to eventually see an evolution where we move away from calling them DATs," said Mitty Chang, chief growth officer at Sora Ventures. "Bitcoin or other digital assets on the balance sheet just become part of a normal treasury operation." Chang noted that his firm's original thesis was never that “you just buy Bitcoin and that’s enough,” but rather that it should be a reserve asset supporting businesses capable of generating revenue.

The frantic search for cash flow is playing out across several distinct avenues:

*   **The Private Equity Play:** Companies like Twenty One Capital are abandoning the passive treasury model to acquire profitable operating businesses in the Bitcoin ecosystem, drawing comparisons to Warren Buffett’s Berkshire Hathaway. Armed with access to public markets, they are looking to buy up exchanges, lending platforms, and infrastructure providers. 
*   **Pivoting to AI and Hard Infrastructure:** To capture the zeitgeist of 2026, many DATs are pivoting into physical energy infrastructure. Firms are acquiring generators and high-voltage transformers to build out Bitcoin mining operations, with some, like Korea's Bit Planet, aggressively weaving artificial intelligence into their corporate narratives to attract capital. 
*   **Traditional Financial Services:** Others are attempting to build comprehensive financial services platforms. Thailand-based Astra has acquired a custodian license and is exploring an asset management license to generate traditional fee-based revenues, while others look to securitize their operating revenues to issue cheaper debt.

### A Forced Hand

The pivot is less of a strategic masterstroke and more of a forced march. The era of easy, accretive capital during the previous bull market is over. Even Michael Saylor’s Strategy—the pioneer of the corporate Bitcoin standard—turned into a net seller over the summer, offloading thousands of tokens to meet cash obligations. 

As Bitcoin enjoys its renaissance at $80,000, the true test of the treasury-company experiment has arrived. The winners of the next cycle may not be the companies that accumulate the most Bitcoin, but those that can build enough underlying cash flow, licenses, and operating businesses to keep their balance sheets alive.
