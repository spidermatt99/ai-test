# Crypto’s 'Treasury' Pioneers Are Flailing for a Second Act Even as Bitcoin Surges

**By Bloomberg News**
**August 28, 2026**

Bitcoin’s return to the $80,000 mark has ignited celebrations across the broader cryptocurrency market, marking the digital asset's best month in nearly two years. But for a specific cohort of companies that built their entire identity around hoarding the token, the mood is decidedly more desperate. 

Digital Asset Treasury companies—or "DATs"—are scrambling to redefine their business models. Initially hailed as innovators for aggressively issuing debt and equity to accumulate Bitcoin on their balance sheets, these companies are now facing a harsh reality: investors are no longer willing to pay a premium just to hold a proxy for the cryptocurrency. 

Despite the recent crypto rally, the DAT sector is still reeling from a massive wipeout. Over $80 billion has been erased from the market capitalizations of top Bitcoin treasury companies since July 2025. Many of these stocks continue to trade at steep discounts to their Net Asset Value (NAV), signaling a total collapse of market faith in the so-called "DAT 1.0" model. 

"The market is telling us that there was a misallocation of capital," said Raphael Zagury, CEO of Twenty One Capital, which holds the world's second-largest Bitcoin corporate treasury. "Raising capital and leveraging the capital is okay, but it's a transaction. The way we're looking at this is much more longer-term."

### The Pivot to 'DAT 3.0'

Faced with a model that Adam Morgan McCarthy, head of research at LO:TECH, described as "always doomed," these companies are frantically pivoting to what insiders are dubbing "DAT 3.0." The new strategy? Actually operating a business.

Instead of passively sitting on digital reserves, DATs are attempting to transition from highly leveraged crypto hedge funds into holding companies, drawing lofty—and perhaps overly ambitious—comparisons to Warren Buffett’s Berkshire Hathaway. 

The frantic search for a new identity is playing out across several distinct avenues:

*   **The Private Equity Play:** Companies like Twenty One Capital are abandoning the passive treasury model to acquire cash-flowing, profitable operating businesses in the Bitcoin ecosystem. Armed with access to public markets, they are looking to buy up exchanges, lending platforms, and infrastructure providers. 
*   **Pivoting to AI and Hard Infrastructure:** To capture the zeitgeist of 2026, many DATs are pivoting hard into physical energy infrastructure. Firms are acquiring generators and high-voltage transformers to build out Bitcoin mining operations, with some, like Korea's Bit Planet, aggressively weaving artificial intelligence into their corporate narratives to attract capital. 
*   **Traditional Financial Services:** Others are attempting to build comprehensive financial services platforms. Thailand-based Astra has acquired a custodian license and is pursuing an asset management license to generate traditional fee-based revenues. Meanwhile, others are exploring securitizing the revenues of their operating companies to issue cheaper debt.

### A Forced Hand

The pivot is less of a strategic masterstroke and more of a forced march. The era of easy, accretive capital during the previous bull market is over. Even Michael Saylor’s Strategy—the pioneer of the corporate Bitcoin standard—turned into a net seller over the summer, offloading thousands of tokens to meet cash obligations for its interest-bearing instruments. 

For the hundreds of smaller copycats—ranging from Japanese clothing retailers to Spanish coffee chains—that bolted Bitcoin onto their balance sheets to juice their stock prices, the music has stopped. Many are quietly dumping their tokens and reverting to their original, less glamorous operations. 

"If you are going to tap investors all around the world, you need to give them that bigger picture because otherwise it’s going to get harder and harder to fund the accumulation," noted Eric Benoist, a research specialist at Natixis CIB.

As Bitcoin enjoys its renaissance at $80,000, the DAT sector serves as a lingering cautionary tale from the last cycle: holding an asset is not a substitute for having a business.
