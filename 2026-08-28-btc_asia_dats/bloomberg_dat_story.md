# Trump Crypto Adviser's 97% Wipeout Shows How Bitcoin Hoarders Are Flailing for a Second Act

**By Bloomberg News**
**August 28, 2026**

The party was back at Bitcoin Asia this week as the token notched its best month in nearly two years, but the biggest crypto hoarders were missing out on the fun. 

Digital Asset Treasury companies—or "DATs"—are nursing a massive hangover. The model of aggressively issuing shares and debt to perpetually hoard digital tokens has come under severe strain, with over $80 billion erased from the market capitalizations of top Bitcoin treasury companies over the last 12 months. 

Now, the survivors are frantically searching for a new business model, shifting away from passively holding Bitcoin toward acquiring cash-flowing operations.

The somber mood at the conference stood in sharp contrast to the broader crypto market, which has been celebrating Bitcoin’s recent surge back to the $80,000 mark. It is an even sharper reversal from the euphoria of last year's event. 

In August 2025, cryptographer and DAT founder Adam Back famously declared to the Bitcoin Asia audience that "eventually, probably all companies will be Bitcoin treasury companies." Today, the companies that built their entire identities around the asset are being left behind and forced to fundamentally rethink their existence.

### The Hunt for Cash Flow

David Bailey knows the market is testing him. Over the past 12 months, shares in Nakamoto—his high-profile attempt to build a massive corporate Bitcoin treasury—have plummeted by roughly 97%. In March, Nakamoto was even forced to sell roughly $20 million of its Bitcoin at a loss to fund operations.

Despite the bloodbath, the prominent crypto advocate and informal adviser to Donald Trump’s presidential campaign insists he isn't capitulating. "I have absolutely zero plan to liquidate Nakamoto," Bailey told Bloomberg. "I plan on running this thing for the next 35 years."

But to survive, Nakamoto is drastically altering its playbook, aggressively hunting for cash-flowing businesses to acquire. 

"We want to be building our Bitcoin balance sheet as much as possible, and we want to be well positioned for the bull market," Bailey said. "But cash flow helps you take risk. We can't control the price of Bitcoin. We can control building useful products and services."

Faced with a model that many analysts now view as doomed, the industry is pivoting to what insiders call "DAT 3.0." Companies are abandoning the passive treasury model to acquire profitable operating businesses in the Bitcoin ecosystem. 

"The market is telling us that there was a misallocation of capital," said Raphael Zagury, CEO of Twenty One Capital, which holds the world's second-largest corporate Bitcoin treasury. "The way we're looking to build our treasury and to give shareholder value in the long term is by finding good operating companies in the Bitcoin ecosystem. Similar, in a sense, to what Berkshire Hathaway did."

### A Risky Pivot

But pivoting away from the original DAT playbook is no guarantee of salvation, and earlier attempts to rebrand have already ended in failure. 

Alt5 Sigma—which once held tokens of World Liberty Financial, the Trump family’s crypto venture—changed its name to AI Financial Corporation in April in what it pitched to investors as a pivot to artificial intelligence. Since the pivot, the stock has dropped another 30%. 

Others haven't survived long enough to attempt a pivot. In July, shareholders of Satsuma Technology Plc voted to completely liquidate the company’s remaining Bitcoin and delist.

### The Counter Example

Not everyone is convinced that M&A or operating businesses are the answer. Strive, another major player in the space, is adamantly sticking to its roots in the capital markets rather than chasing cash flow or putting its Bitcoin at risk to generate yield.

Strive uses financial engineering—specifically a perpetual preferred equity instrument that pays a 13% annual return—to manage its capital structure without selling its holdings. 

"We have access to the capital markets. We could do yield generation strategies. That's something we've considered, but most yield generation strategies create a risk profile that is not aligned with our assets," said Strive Chief Risk Officer Jeff Walton. 

Walton noted that lending Bitcoin as collateral could lead to catastrophic losses if the market suddenly turns. "If the price of Bitcoin moves down 30%, I lose the Bitcoin. So instead, I'm not using Bitcoin as collateral. I'm using my balance sheet as collateral."

As the sector continues to flail for a second act, the struggles of the broader DAT ecosystem underscore a central tension: believing an asset will ultimately rise is not the same thing as having the business model to survive until it does.
