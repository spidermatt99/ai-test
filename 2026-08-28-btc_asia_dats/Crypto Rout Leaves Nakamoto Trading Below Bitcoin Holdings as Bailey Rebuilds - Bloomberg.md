A year ago, David Bailey appeared to have the Midas touch. The crypto entrepreneur who helped win Donald Trump over to Bitcoin had [raised](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250620361457/en/KindlyMD-and-Nakamoto-Announce-an-Additional-%2451.5-Million-In-PIPE-Financing-To-Support-Bitcoin-Treasury-Efforts "link") about $760 million for a public company built to stockpile the cryptocurrency, while his hedge fund had returned 640% backing similar bets.

Since the May 2025 announcement of the merger that created the public-market vehicle for Bailey’s [Nakamoto Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/NAKA:US), shares have plummeted about 99% from their peak, part of a broader rout in digital-asset treasury companies that has wiped out the premium investors once paid to own Bitcoin through publicly traded companies. Bailey, who says he remains periodically in touch with the White House, is now trying to prove there is a viable business underneath the collapse.

“It’s been a very rough year for us,” Bailey said in an interview at this year’s Bitcoin Asia conference. “We bought Bitcoin at the top, and then Bitcoin performed very poorly.”

The immediate problem is that the financing engine has broken down. Nakamoto now trades well below the value of its Bitcoin holdings, making it far harder to issue stock to buy more of the cryptocurrency. Bailey is instead turning to a more conventional source of firepower: businesses that generate cash.

Nakamoto is hunting for another acquisition that can improve earnings and help finance future Bitcoin purchases without repeatedly issuing shares or taking on more debt. It is also prioritizing repurchases of its own discounted stock over adding to its Bitcoin holdings. In March, Nakamoto was even forced to sell roughly $20 million of its Bitcoin at a loss to fund operations, selling at an average price of about $70,000—well below its weighted-average purchase price of $118,171.

This pullback contrasts sharply with Michael Saylor's Strategy Inc., the largest crypto treasury firm, which only recently paused its Bitcoin buying after 13 straight weeks of increasing its holdings.

“We’re looking at businesses that can help us improve our income and our cash flow at the company,” Bailey said. “We’re looking at businesses that have strategic value to Bitcoin as a whole.”

Despite the challenges, Bailey insists he isn't capitulating. "I have absolutely zero plan to liquidate Nakamoto," he told Bloomberg. "I plan on running this thing for the next 35 years." He added: "We want to be building our Bitcoin balance sheet as much as possible, and we want to be well positioned for the bull market. But cash flow helps you take risk. We can't control the price of Bitcoin. We can control building useful products and services."

Nakamoto’s shares fell so far that the company carried out a 1-for-40 reverse split in May to regain compliance with Nasdaq’s minimum bid-price requirement. The collapse has also drawn criticism from retail investors and Bitcoin devotees who bought into the company during the boom.

Skepticism intensified in February when Nakamoto used stock to [buy](https://nakamoto.com/updates/nakamoto-inc-completes-acquisition-of-btc-inc-and-utxo-management "Website") two Bailey-led companies, a media outfit and an investment firm. The deal increased the fully diluted share count by about 70%, diluting existing shareholders’ ownership, a transaction that short seller Jim Chanos [called](https://x.com/RealJimChanos/status/2023828158047547661?s=20 "Website") “Theater of the Absurd.” For the first half of 2026, Nakamoto reported losses of roughly $372 million.

“It was always our plan to merge them in,” Bailey said of the criticism surrounding the mergers, suggesting investors expected it. “It’s what we told them we were going to do.”

Nakamoto [disclosed](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1946573/000149315225024260/R11.htm "link") last year that it had retained an option to wholly acquire [BTC Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/2087085D:US) within three years.

Bailey has kept [buying](https://nakamoto.com/updates/nakamoto-chairman-and-ceo-david-bailey-purchases-additional-shares-of-common-stock "link") Nakamoto shares himself and says the company is nowhere near finished. His political access has proved more durable: he said his continued contact with senior White House officials has included outreach to the Bitcoin community ahead of November’s midterm elections.

Bitcoin has recently climbed back toward $80,000 after falling below $60,000 earlier this year. Whether that marks the end of the bear market is uncertain.

For Bailey, the more pressing question is whether Nakamoto can survive long enough to benefit from a recovery — and whether investors will ever again pay a premium for a company built largely around owning more Bitcoin. Bailey is hopeful.

“I think the next three years ahead are going to be really fantastic,” Bailey said. “Our team is already dramatically better today than we were nine months ago, and I think we’re going to be dramatically better nine months from now.”
